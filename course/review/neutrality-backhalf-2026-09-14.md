# Global coverage and evidence review: modules 11–21 and surveys 06–10

Date: 14 September 2026. Authored lesson text frozen before the coordinated rebuild and narration regeneration.

## Scope and standard

This pass reviewed the eleven deep modules from transistor architectures through the reference directory and the five corresponding survey lessons. It also corrected module 20 quiz questions 4 and 5 with explicit approval. The purpose was to teach the actual global production network without using nationality or export-control exposure as a reason to omit a supplier, and without treating an announced product, a chip teardown, factory yield and market leadership as interchangeable evidence.

The directory remains representative rather than exhaustive. Including an additional supplier does not imply equal capabilities or equal market share. Company headquarters, manufacturing sites, ownership, billing addresses and customer locations are different geographic measures. Product qualification and the limits of manufacturer evidence apply to every company, rather than appearing as a special warning attached only to Chinese entries.

## Explicit quiz amendments

Module 20 retains eight questions, four choices per question and all existing correct-answer indices. Only questions 4 and 5 were rewritten; the coordinator approved the exact new options and applied narrowly scoped QA contract amendments.

**Question 4:** The former question assumed demonstrated “5 nm-class parts” and its correct option asserted yield “well below 50%,” an uneconomic process and that 3 nm-class was not credible. The revised question asks what DUV multi-patterning changes and what a teardown can establish. Correct choice remains index **2**:

> Additional patterning and alignment steps can increase cost, cycle time and defect exposure; a teardown can establish dimensions and structures, but does not establish factory yield or cost per good die

The explanation preserves the process tradeoff and the documented N+2/N+3 evidence. The change removes unsupported private manufacturing outcomes from the test’s required answer.

**Question 5:** The former question asked learners to accept about 90% of both advanced logic and “CoWoS-class” packaging, plus a precise blockade timeline. The revised question teaches supply dependence and product qualification. Correct choice remains index **1**:

> An interruption to qualified chemicals, gases, spare parts or transport can stop output; alternative foundries and packaging technologies require product-specific redesign and qualification

The explanation distinguishes TSMC’s branded process family from other advanced packaging. It preserves the risk to intact factories without requiring an unsupported universal packaging denominator or shutdown timetable.
